Identity system · v1.0

Halo Energy brand guidelines.

Intelligent, precise, dependable and forward-looking. This page is the visual reference for everyone who designs for Halo. Full written guidance is in docs/02-identity-guidelines.md.

01 · Logo concepts

Three directions. One recommended.

Recommended · Concept A

Orbit Node

A solid core (the physical asset) inside an open orbit (the intelligence around it). A single mint node sits in the opening of the ring — the point where an asset becomes connected. The open ring reads as a halo without becoming a literal circle, and implies continuous movement.

Why it wins: simplest geometry, survives at 16 px, works as a single-colour stencil on PPE and machinery, and its parts map directly onto the brand idea. The node also becomes a reusable graphic device (status dots, bullets, map pins).

Concept B

Horizon

A halo arc rising from a ground line — intelligence emerging from physical infrastructure, with a second line suggesting a pipeline or road corridor. Strong industrial grounding and a sunrise reading that suits an African-born growth story.

Trade-off: leans toward conventional energy imagery and loses some of the technology signal at small sizes.

Concept C

Interlink

An H monogram whose crossbar is replaced by an orbital ellipse: two pillars (equipment and infrastructure) held together by an intelligence loop, with a node on its path.

Trade-off: distinctive as a monogram and strong on vehicles, but more complex and more reliant on the letter H than on the halo idea.

02 · Logo system

Primary logo and variations.

Use the horizontal lock-up by default. Use the stacked version where width is limited and the symbol alone at small sizes or as a stamp.

Do

  • Keep clear space equal to the diameter of the core on every side.
  • Minimum size: symbol 16 px / 5 mm; horizontal logo 120 px / 30 mm wide.
  • Use the single-colour mark for embroidery, stencils, etching and decals.
  • Use the mint node only in full-colour versions.

Don't

  • Close the ring, rotate the mark or move the node.
  • Recolour the node amber, or place the logo on busy photography without a scrim.
  • Add effects — gradients, glows, outlines or shadows.
  • Pair the symbol with any wordmark other than the supplied lock-ups.

Approximate proportion of use

Signal Mint fails contrast as text on light backgrounds. On Mist or white use Mint Ink #0B7A64 for text and links, and Amber Ink #9A5310 for amber text.

04 · Typography

Engineered, wide and legible.

Display · Archivo (expanded)
Aa

Headlines, numbers and the wordmark. Bold 700, width 108–120%, tight tracking (−2 to −3%).

Text · IBM Plex Sans
Aa

Body copy, UI and forms. Regular 400, Medium 500, SemiBold 600. 16–18 px body, 1.6 line height.

Data · IBM Plex Mono
Aa

Labels, eyebrows, data and technical annotations. Uppercase, tracked +10–12%.

All three families are open-source (SIL Open Font License) and free for print, web and signage.

06 · Photography direction

Real sites. Real people. Honest light.

  • Commission, don't borrowShoot real Halo-coordinated equipment, partners and sites. Never present stock or other companies' projects as Halo work.
  • Cinematic, not dramaticLow early-morning or late-afternoon light, wide frames, machinery in context. Grade cool in shadows to sit on navy.
  • People in full PPEEvery person on site shown in correct PPE, working safely. Feature Nigerian operators, engineers and technicians.
  • Show the data momentTablets during inspections, telematics units, maintenance sign-off — the point where physical meets digital.
  • AvoidFlames, gushing oil, glossy staged handshakes, generic "AI" imagery and overlaid fake dashboards.

Until photography is commissioned, the site uses schematic line illustrations, clearly captioned as illustrations.
